It gets said in good faith, usually in the first hour, usually by someone frightened. *Nothing physical happened.* And it lands like a door closing.

Understanding why is the difference between a repair that works and one that stalls for years.

## It answers a question nobody asked

The hurt partner is not conducting an inventory of body parts. They are asking three things, none of which the phrase touches:

- **Were you choosing someone else?**
- **Were you hiding it from me?**
- **Is what I thought we had still true?**

Answering *it wasn't physical* to any of those reads as a technicality. Worse, it reads as an argument — and the moment betrayal turns into an argument, the person who was hurt is now doing two jobs: managing the pain, and managing your defence of it.

## For many people it is the worse betrayal

There is a common assumption that physical infidelity is the serious kind and emotional infidelity is the lesser one. Plenty of people experience it the other way around.

A one-night encounter can be understood as a failure of judgement. Six months of daily messages, private jokes, and being someone's first thought in the morning is not a lapse. It is **a relationship**, conducted deliberately, while another one was left to run on empty.

That is why the phrase stings. It minimises the part that actually did the damage.

## What was actually taken

Not sex. Three things, and it helps to be able to name them:

**Attention.** The good hours. The interesting conversation. The version of you that is curious and funny rather than depleted.

**Disclosure.** The things you said to them first. In most relationships, being told first *is* the intimacy.

**Reality.** For however long it went on, your partner was living in a version of their life that was not accurate. People underestimate how disorienting that is. It is not only the loss — it is discovering that the last six months of your own memory need re-reading.

## What to say instead

If you are the one who did it, drop the category defence entirely. It will not help you, and it is costing you the only thing that might.

Try, roughly: *"I know what I took from you. I gave someone else the attention that belonged here, and I let you believe things were fine. You are not overreacting."*

That does two things the defence cannot. It shows you understand the actual injury, and it stops the other person having to prove they were hurt before they are allowed to be.

## If you are the one who was hurt

You do not need the act to have crossed a specific line in order for your reaction to be legitimate. You are allowed to be devastated by something that was never physical. Do not let a definition argument talk you out of your own experience.

You also do not have to decide anything this week. See [what to do the week after you find out](/blog/after-you-find-out).
