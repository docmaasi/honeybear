From inside, they feel the same. Distance, irritation, the sense of living beside someone rather than with them. Everyone wants a test that tells them which one they are in.

There is no clean test. There are signals, and they are not the obvious ones.

## What does not tell you

**Frequency of arguing.** Couples who argue often are frequently fine. Couples who never argue are sometimes finished and quiet about it.

**Sex.** It moves for a hundred reasons — illness, small children, stress, medication. Its absence is information, not a verdict.

**Not feeling in love this month.** Feelings move. Long relationships have flat stretches, some of them a year long.

## What does tell you

**Contempt.** Not anger — contempt. Eye-rolling, mockery, a tone that says *you are beneath discussing this with*. This is the single most corrosive thing that can be present, and unlike anger it does not resolve on its own.

**Curiosity gone.** You have stopped wondering what they think. When someone becomes fully predictable to you and you are relieved rather than sad about that, something significant has already left.

**Repair has stopped.** Rough patches still contain repair — apologies, reaching back, someone breaking the silence. When nobody bothers reaching any more, that is the meaningful change.

**Relief at their absence.** Not enjoying an evening alone. Relief. Consistently. Notice the difference honestly.

**You have stopped imagining them in the future.** Not planning it — imagining it. If your mental picture of five years from now quietly stopped including them some time ago, that is worth knowing.

## The question that cuts through

> **If nothing changed at all, could I live like this for another ten years, and would I want to?**

A rough patch answers *no, but it will change*. An ending answers *no, and I no longer believe it will* — and there is usually a moment of recognition on that second one.

## Before deciding

**Check what is actually going on.** Grief, illness, a new baby, money fear and burnout all imitate a dying relationship convincingly. Fix the sleep before you rewrite the marriage.

**Try being the one who moves first.** Six weeks of genuinely different behaviour from you — more warmth, less scorekeeping. Not as a trap to prove they will not reciprocate. As an experiment.

**Say the true thing out loud.** A surprising number of couples in this state have never once said plainly: *I am unhappy, and I want us to fix it.* They have hinted for years.

**Get a third person in the room.** A counsellor is not an admission of defeat. It is someone whose job is to interrupt the pattern the two of you cannot see.

## If it is an ending

Ending something honestly is not a failure. A relationship that ran its course and closed cleanly is a better outcome than one that decayed for a decade while both people pretended.

## One exception

If what you feel is fear rather than sadness — if you are managing your behaviour to avoid a reaction — this article does not apply to your situation. Read [early signs of a controlling relationship](/blog/control-signs) instead.
