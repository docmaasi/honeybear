It is usually phrased carefully. *There is someone I talk to. It is not physical. Nothing has happened. My spouse does not know.*

Every clause there is doing work. Let us take them honestly, because the people asking are not villains — they are usually lonely, and telling themselves something that has stopped being true.

## What people tell themselves

**"It is not real."** It is real enough to hide, real enough to think about during the day, and real enough that losing it would hurt. That is a relationship. The medium is a detail.

**"Nothing has happened."** Something has happened. Hours of attention and disclosure have moved. The absence of a body does not make it nothing; it makes it a different kind of something.

**"It is keeping my marriage together."** Occasionally people mean this sincerely — the outside relationship absorbs a need and reduces the pressure at home. What it actually does is **remove the pressure that would have forced the real conversation.** The marriage does not get repaired. It gets anaesthetised.

**"My spouse would not understand."** Probably true. That is not a reason it is fine. It is a description of why it is a betrayal.

## What is actually happening

Two things at once, and both are worth saying plainly.

Something in the marriage went unmet long before anyone opened a headset. That is real, and it usually is not one person's fault.

And a second relationship is now being funded with attention, honesty and desire that used to belong to the first. The spouse is experiencing that loss without being told there is a reason for it. That is the specific cruelty of it — not the other person, but leaving someone to conclude they are simply less lovable than they used to be.

## Family patterns underneath

A lot of this traces back further than the marriage. People who grew up watching a parent conduct a quiet second life, or watching two adults keep a peace by never saying the true thing, tend to reproduce the arrangement without ever choosing it.

Katherine's book **Weight of Dysfunction** works through exactly this ground — what a family teaches without saying it, and what it takes to be the person who stops a pattern rather than passing it along. It is [available on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Weight-Dysfunction-Katherine-L-Carter/dp/1667889222), and it is the reason this subject sits so close to the centre of the show.

## The three roads

There are only three, and drifting is not one of them, though most people try it.

**End the outside relationship and do the real work.** Hardest, and the only one that leaves the marriage intact and honest.

**Tell your spouse and let them choose.** Frightening, and it restores something you have been taking from them: a say in their own life.

**End the marriage.** Sometimes the truthful answer. Doing it cleanly is kinder than a slow disappearance.
