Everyone knows the feeling. Twenty minutes in and something clicks. It feels like recognition, like meeting someone you already knew.

That feeling is real. It is also one of the least reliable pieces of information available, and treating it as a verdict causes a great deal of damage.

## What chemistry is often made of

Sometimes it is genuine compatibility arriving fast. Frequently it is one of these instead:

**Familiarity.** The strongest instant chemistry often comes from someone who matches an emotional pattern you already know. If what you know is inconsistency, then inconsistency will feel like electricity and steadiness will feel like nothing at all.

**Uncertainty.** Not knowing where you stand produces a physiological state that is almost indistinguishable from excitement. A great deal of what people call chemistry is simply an unresolved question.

**Being chosen.** Sudden intense interest is flattering, and flattery is easy to mistake for connection. Notice whether you like *them*, or like how you feel when they look at you.

**Skill.** Some people are genuinely good at this. Being effortlessly easy to talk to on a first meeting is a competence, and it is not evidence of anything else.

## The uncomfortable pattern

People with difficult histories often report the strongest chemistry with the people who go on to hurt them, and describe the kind, steady person as *nice, but no spark*.

That is not bad luck and it is not a character flaw. It is a nervous system recognising a familiar shape and reporting it as home. Familiar and good are different words, and for some people they have never once overlapped.

If that describes you, it is worth taking seriously rather than fighting. Katherine's **Weight of Dysfunction**, [available on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Weight-Dysfunction-Katherine-L-Carter/dp/1667889222), sits on exactly this ground — what you learned to expect before you were old enough to have a say.

## What actually predicts things

**Warmth that is stable across weeks**, rather than intensity that spikes.

**Ease.** Not fireworks — the absence of effort. Not having to manage yourself.

**Repair.** How a first disagreement goes tells you more than a hundred good evenings.

**Whether you like who you are around them.** This is the whole thing, and people consistently underrate it.

## What to do with it

Do not ignore chemistry. It matters, and a relationship with none is hard to sustain. Just demote it from verdict to data point.

Two practical rules:

**Give the slow build a real chance.** Attraction that grows over two months is more predictive than attraction that arrived complete on night one. Three or four meetings, not one.

**Treat instant, overwhelming certainty as a reason to slow down.** Not to run — to slow down. The strongest feeling in the room is not automatically the truest thing in it.

## The short version

Chemistry tells you something is happening. It does not tell you what. [The first six weeks](/blog/first-six-weeks) is where you find out.

## A useful habit

After a first meeting, write two sentences: what you actually liked about them, and what you actually felt. They are different questions, and separating them is the fastest way to notice when you are excited by a situation rather than a person.
