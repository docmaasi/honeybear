Abuse is often imagined as something that requires a room. It does not. Control works perfectly well through a phone, a headset, or an account, and for many people it now arrives that way first.

If any of this is familiar, help exists and it is free. **National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233**, or text START to 88788. It is confidential and available at any hour.

## What it looks like

**Monitoring.** Demanding location at all times. Checking your phone. Requiring your passwords. Installing something to watch you. Wanting to know who you spoke to and for how long.

**Controlling access.** Deciding who you may follow, message, or spend time with. Manufacturing conflict when you see friends until it is easier not to.

**Punishment through contact.** Twenty messages in ten minutes. Silence for two days as a penalty. Contact used as a lever rather than a conversation.

**Threatening exposure.** Using photographs or private messages as leverage. This has a name — image-based abuse — and in most places it is a crime.

**Impersonation and account control.** Using your accounts, posting as you, controlling your money through a shared app.

**Reality management.** Denying messages you both read. Editing what was said. Telling you repeatedly that you are remembering it wrong.

## Why it is hard to name

Because each piece has a reasonable-sounding explanation, and because it usually arrives dressed as love. *I just worry. I want to know you're safe. If you had nothing to hide it wouldn't matter.*

And because there are no marks. Many people carry this for years without using the word abuse, because nobody ever hit them.

**Fear is the test, not injury.** If you are managing your own behaviour to avoid someone's reaction, that is the relevant fact, whatever the mechanism.

## In virtual spaces

The same behaviours appear in VR and voice platforms, with additions: demanding you stay in one world, tracking which rooms you enter, requiring you to stay in a private space, following you into every gathering, threatening to expose an online relationship to people offline.

Every major platform has blocking and reporting. Use them. You are not obliged to be reachable.

## Practical safety

- **Check your devices** on a machine they cannot access, ideally somewhere else. A library computer, a friend's phone.
- **Change passwords from a safe device**, and check what is set to sync.
- **Look at location sharing** in your phone settings and in individual apps.
- **Keep a record.** Screenshots with dates, stored somewhere they cannot reach — an account they do not know about, or with someone you trust.
- **Do not announce that you are documenting.** It raises risk.

Sudden changes can escalate things. If you are unsure, phone the hotline first and ask. That is exactly what it is for.
