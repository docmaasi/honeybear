Your partner puts on a headset most evenings. They have friends in there you have never met, a room they go to, in-jokes you do not have. When you ask about it you get an answer that is technically complete and tells you nothing.

Is that a problem? Genuinely: not necessarily. But it is worth knowing which one you are looking at.

## When it is fine

People are allowed their own worlds. A partner with somewhere that is theirs — friends of their own, an interest you do not share — is usually a **healthier** partner, not a threatening one.

Signs it is fine:

- They talk about it easily when asked.
- You could come along and nobody would panic.
- It has a shape. It starts and it ends.
- You are not being replaced by it. You still get evenings.

That is a hobby. Golf did this for decades and nobody wrote articles about it.

## When it is something else

The pattern changes when the second world starts taking things from the first.

- **Secrecy rather than privacy.** Privacy is *I don't narrate every conversation*. Secrecy is closing the laptop when you walk in.
- **A steady drain.** Sleep, money, attention, sex, chores. The other world is being funded by this one.
- **You are being managed.** Questions get met with defensiveness or a subject change rather than an answer.
- **Somebody specific.** Not a group any more. One person, whose name keeps coming up, or has stopped coming up.
- **You are the problem for asking.** This is the one to pay attention to.

## The conversation

Do not open with an accusation, because you will get a defence rather than the truth. Open with what you actually feel.

*"I have noticed I don't see much of you in the evenings any more, and I miss you. I'm not asking you to stop. I'd like to understand what it gives you."*

That is hard to argue with, and the response tells you a lot. Someone with nothing to hide will usually be relieved to talk about it. Someone hiding something will make the conversation about your tone.

## If it is an affair

Then it is an affair, and the fact that it happened through a headset does not change much. See [is it cheating if it is only an avatar?](/blog/avatar-cheating) and [what to do the week after you find out](/blog/after-you-find-out).

## If it is not, but you are still lonely

That is real and it counts. Loneliness inside a relationship does not need a betrayal to justify it. Say it plainly, without needing to prove wrongdoing first — the goal is not to win a case, it is to be less alone.

The couples who come out of this well are usually the ones who talked about it in month two rather than month twenty.

## If nothing is wrong

Sometimes the answer is that your partner has a hobby and you are lonely, and both are true at once. That still deserves a conversation. Loneliness inside a relationship does not need a wrongdoer to be worth solving.
