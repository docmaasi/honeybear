Warning lists are everywhere. Almost nobody can describe what *good* looks like, which is a problem — because if you only know what to avoid, you can end up choosing the absence of alarm rather than the presence of anything.

These are the signs worth watching for.

## They repair quickly

Not that they never upset you. That when it happens, the gap between rupture and repair is short. They come back. They say the thing. They do not need three days of silence to regulate.

This is probably the strongest single indicator in the whole list, because every long relationship is a very long series of small ruptures.

## They are the same person in every room

With your friends, with their mother, with a stranger, alone with you at midnight. Not identical in mood — consistent in character. People who change shape depending on the audience are exhausting to be near, and eventually you cannot tell which version you are getting.

## They can be wrong without it costing them anything

Watch what happens when they are shown to be mistaken about something small. Does it land lightly? Someone who cannot be wrong about a restaurant will not be able to be wrong about something that matters.

## They have a life

Friends of their own, an interest that is theirs, somewhere they go. Someone with a full life is choosing you rather than needing you, and the difference becomes very significant over years.

## They notice things

That you were quiet. That you have said the same thing twice. That the anniversary of something difficult is coming up. Attention is not a personality trait people advertise, and it is worth more than almost anything they could say.

## They say what they want

Directly, without hinting or sulking. This is much rarer than it should be, and its absence is the engine of an enormous amount of unnecessary misery.

## They are kind when nobody is grading them

Kindness with an audience is manners. Kindness with no witness is character.

## They can hear something hard without collapsing or attacking

You raise a concern. They neither fall apart, requiring you to comfort them about your own complaint, nor go on the offensive. They stay in the room and consider it. This one is rare enough that when you meet it, take it seriously.

## They are pleased when good things happen to you

Genuinely, immediately, without a competitive undertow. Many relationships survive difficulty and are quietly destroyed by one person's success.

## Why the list matters

People who grew up around chaos often mistake *not alarming* for *good*. If your baseline was volatility, calm can register as boring and someone steady can feel like nothing at all.

That is a learned response, not a preference, and it is worth knowing about yourself. Katherine writes about that inheritance in **Weight of Dysfunction**, [available on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Weight-Dysfunction-Katherine-L-Carter/dp/1667889222) — how a family teaches you what to expect, long before you have any say in it.

If several of these are present, pay attention. That combination is not common, and the absence of red flags is not the same thing as the presence of these.
