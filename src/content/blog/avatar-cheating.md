This is the most common question the show gets, and it is usually asked hoping for one of two answers. Here is the one that is actually useful.

**The avatar is not the test.** Whether pixels were involved decides nothing. Three other things decide it.

## The three questions that matter

**1. Was it secret?**

Not private — secret. Would you have been comfortable if your partner had seen the whole thing as it happened? If the honest answer is no, that is not a technicality. Secrecy is the part that does the damage, more reliably than any act.

**2. Was intimacy redirected?**

Every relationship runs on a finite supply of attention, disclosure, and desire. If the things you used to bring home are now being spent elsewhere, your partner is experiencing a loss whether or not anyone touched anyone. They are not imagining the absence.

**3. Did it break an agreement?**

Explicit or understood. Most couples never sat down and defined this, which is precisely why it goes wrong. Someone assumed a line existed. Someone else assumed it did not.

If the answer to all three is yes, then it functions as an affair, and arguing about the medium is a way of avoiding the conversation.

## Why "it wasn't real" does not land

People reach for this defence honestly. It rarely helps, because it answers a question the hurt partner is not asking.

They are not asking whether you had a body somewhere. They are asking: *were you choosing someone else, and hiding it from me?* Answering the wrong question reads as evasion even when it is not meant that way. There is more on this in [why "it wasn't physical" isn't the defence people think](/blog/not-physical).

## The genuinely grey areas

Not everything is clear, and pretending otherwise is useless.

- **A flirty friendship that never escalates.** Depends entirely on secrecy and redirection.
- **Roleplay inside a game, understood as a game.** Very often fine, if both partners know and nobody is quietly investing.
- **An ex you talk to in a virtual world.** The medium is irrelevant. The relationship is not.
- **Sexual content with strangers.** Some couples are genuinely fine with this. Others are not. What matters is whether you actually agreed, rather than assumed.

The grey is not a loophole. It is a signal that a conversation is overdue.

## What to do instead of litigating it

Have the boring conversation *before* you need it. Sit down and answer, out loud:

- What counts as crossing a line for each of us?
- What are we each fine with that the other might not be?
- What would we want to be told, and how soon?

Almost no couple does this. It takes twenty minutes and prevents a category of pain that otherwise takes years to work through.

## If it has already happened

Then the question is not whether it was technically cheating. It is whether you both want to repair it, and whether the person who did it can be honest now. Start with [what to do the week after you find out](/blog/after-you-find-out).
