If dating apps have left you flat, cynical, and slightly worse at conversation, you are not failing at them. You are responding sensibly to a system that is not built to get you what you want.

## Why they wear people down

**Choice destroys attention.** Faced with an apparently infinite queue, nobody invests in the person in front of them. Everyone is holding the door open for someone slightly better.

**They optimise for the wrong moment.** Apps are built around a decision made in under two seconds from a photograph. Almost nothing that determines whether a relationship works is visible in that window.

**Rejection arrives without information.** Ordinary social rejection comes with context. App rejection is silence, at volume, and the mind fills silence with the least generous explanation available.

**They are engagement businesses.** A product whose revenue depends on your continued use is not straightforwardly incentivised to end your need for it.

None of that means apps never work. Plenty of good relationships started on one. It means the burnout is a reasonable reaction rather than a personal deficiency.

## What actually works

**Repeated exposure through activity.** Almost every durable relationship starts in a place people returned to — a class, a team, a volunteer shift, a regular room. Repetition lets people see each other unperformed, which is the only way anyone assesses anyone accurately.

This is exactly why virtual worlds have become such effective meeting places. A weekly room where the same people gather is structurally the same thing as a Tuesday night class. **HoneyBear Katherine's Haven** runs live in Meta Horizon Worlds and Clubroom every week, and a fair number of friendships have started there simply through people turning up to the same thing repeatedly.

**Being introduced.** Someone vouched for is pre-verified in a way no profile can be.

**Being somewhere with a purpose that is not dating.** The pressure drops, and people become recognisably themselves.

## If you are staying on the apps

- **Choose one, not four.** Spread attention is what makes it feel like a job.
- **Move to a real conversation quickly.** Voice or in person within a week. Text chemistry is its own skill and predicts very little.
- **Stop optimising the profile.** Diminishing returns arrive fast. Be legible rather than impressive.
- **Set a limit.** Twenty minutes a day. Unbounded scrolling is where the corrosion happens.
- **Take real breaks.** A month off is not defeat; it is maintenance.

## The bigger reframe

The goal is not to find someone efficiently. It is to become someone with a life full enough that meeting people is a by-product of living it.

That sounds like a consolation. It is not — it is the mechanism. People who are genuinely occupied by something meet more people, choose better, and are considerably more attractive to be near, because they are not auditioning.

When you do meet someone, [the first six weeks](/blog/first-six-weeks) is what to pay attention to.

## One last thing

If you have been at this a while and it has made you cynical, that cynicism is the real cost, more than the wasted evenings. It follows you into the next good thing and quietly spoils it. Take the break before it hardens into a worldview.
