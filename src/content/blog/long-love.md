Ask a couple at thirty years what the secret is and you rarely get anything useful. They shrug. They say you just keep going.

Watch them for a week and you see what they cannot articulate. It is a small set of habits, and almost none of it is romantic.

## They turn toward small bids

All day, people make tiny requests for attention. *Look at this. Did you see that. I had a strange dream.* Trivial, constantly, mostly unnoticed.

The couples who last **answer** them. Not with a speech — a look up, a hum, a question. The ones who drift are usually not fighting. They have simply been not answering for years, and one day there is nothing left to answer.

This is the least glamorous and most predictive thing on the list.

## They repair fast

Not fewer arguments. A shorter gap between the rupture and someone reaching back. The couple that fights hard and repairs in an hour is in better shape than the couple that is cold for four days.

If nobody in your family ever modelled repair, this has to be learned deliberately. [How to apologise so it lands](/blog/apology) is the mechanics.

## They are on the same side of the problem

The problem is on one side and the two of you are on the other. Not *you versus me about the money* but *us versus the money*.

Couples who lose this start collecting evidence about each other, and once a relationship becomes a case being built, the outcome is largely decided.

## They let each other change

Nobody is the same person at fifty as at thirty. Couples that last let the other one become someone new without treating it as a breach of contract. Couples that fail keep trying to talk each other back into an earlier version.

## They keep some separateness

Own friends, own interests, own inner life. Not distance — separateness. Two people who have entirely merged have nothing to bring back to each other, and interest requires some distance to exist across.

## They say the true thing early

Not everything. The true thing. Resentment mostly comes from things that were survivable when small and were left for two years.

## They are affectionate without an agenda

Touch that is not a negotiation about something else. Hands, shoulders, passing contact. It does more work than almost anything people consciously try.

## What is not on the list

Constant passion. Never being bored. Never fancying anyone else. Shared hobbies. Being each other's only friend. Never doubting it.

All of those are optional, and treating them as requirements has ended a considerable number of relationships that were working fine.

## The uncomfortable one

Long relationships are not sustained by feeling. They are sustained by **behaviour that continues while the feeling is temporarily absent**. Everyone has flat months. The ones who last keep answering the small bids anyway, and the feeling comes back, because the habits were still running underneath it.

That is not unromantic. It is the least sentimental and most reliable description of love there is.
